Why Professional HVAC Maintenance Matters

Homeowner maintenance is useful but limited: filters changed on schedule, the outdoor coil kept clear, registers open, drains glanced at. Everything else requires instruments, because the failures that strand you do not announce themselves visually. A capacitor at 80 percent of rating looks identical to a healthy one; only a meter knows. A charge that drifted low reads only in superheat and subcooling. A heat exchanger crack, a lazy flame signal, a pressure switch on the edge, an amp draw creeping toward the breaker's limit: every one of these is invisible until it is measured, and every one is cheaper the season before it fails.

Once gas is involved, maintenance becomes a safety issue. A failing burner, flame proving circuit, vent, or draft problem can put combustion products where they do not belong, which is why those items get instrumented verification every year. How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Flush the condensate line every spring so algae cannot build up and clog it
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly if any of your equipment burns fuel
  • Trim plants back from the outdoor unit and give the coil a gentle rinse every spring
  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
  • Note new sounds and longer run times; they are data your technician can use
  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Resist closing vents in spare rooms; the system is designed with all registers open
