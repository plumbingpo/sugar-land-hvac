How El Paso Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Many mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that point and also shows higher bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or frequent repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install in peak season.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
